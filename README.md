# No Mans Land

A small auto-battler for the phone browser. Buy squads, place them, and watch the armies fight. Play the AI or challenge a friend live.

- **Tutorial:** four guided rounds of one match that build the screen up step by step: your deployment zone blinking, your tower ("Protect it"), one card, a tile, Fight. The enemy's hidden Skeleton Archers win round 1 (how damage works); in round 2 their army is in sight and Fire Mage, which beats crowds, wins it. Round 3 opens up a little: the tower pick (Bastion or Keep only, a line on each; `m.towerOffer` fixes the offer) and its skill (a glowing panel button), a shop of five basic units with the deployment cap (the enemy adds a hidden Brute). Round 4: a level-up (its sheet also has Move, Sell and skills), the flanks and no man's land. Then the enemy plays for real, as the AI, with the full shop and the clock, and one coach line on that first screen says so. Tutorial fights play at 1x; the X on the coach line leaves. The menu leads with it until it's been played through round 4 (saved on the device), then it's a small button. The script is `TUT` and `panelTutorial()` in `src/site-template.html`; `tools/tests/tutorial-ui.js` checks it on both boards. Rounds 1 and 2 come out the same wherever the player places (checked headlessly over the zone's tiles), so after changing those four units' stats, check the script still holds.
- **Player health:** each player starts with 100. The loser of a round loses health for every enemy squad still standing (by tier: 3 / 6 / 8 / 12, `TIER_DMG`), but never more than 35 a round (`MAX_ROUND_DMG` in `src/game.js`). A player whose tower falls in a fight also loses 10 health (`TOWER_FALL_HP`).
- **Economy (2026-10-28, against waiting for gold and then massing tier 4):** the loser's +50 gold only from round 3 (`LOSER_BONUS_ROUND`). Everyone may take a loan once a round on every map from round 1: +100 now, 200 less next round (`LOAN`); the Bank tower's Credit line makes it +200 / 300 (`CREDIT_LINE`, `loanTerms`), and a bank still does one thing a round. Major investment: pay 500, get 800 next round, once per match. The bot only borrows with a Credit line. `tools/tests/economy-1028.js` checks all of it.
- **Towers (2026-10-28):** Keep: Muster (trait: the extra deployment is free, `deployCost`), Brute guards 400. Sniper tower: Deploy airship 400, the airship shoots like a level 3 Sniper (`AIRSHIP.level`); Mark Bounty (`MARK`, 50 once): one enemy squad from an earlier round is marked for the match (`m.mark[side] = { squad, round }`, hidden from them until that fight), Snipers deal it +15% and see it through stealth (engine `sniperMark` / `marked`), and a fight where a Sniper takes its last body pays 100 (`res.markKill`); it can't be moved, and if the squad is sold it can be placed again for free. Bastion: Fortify (`FORTIFY`, 50 a round: one wall for this round's fight only, a `temp` wall in `m.bwalls`) and Vault (trait: treasury +6 armor, raids take half, `VAULT`). The Overwatch skill also sees stealthed units within its range (`u.trueSight`). The bot marks the priciest enemy squad and sometimes fortifies. `tools/tests/towers-1028.js`.
- **Encyclopedia:** a start-screen button opening Units, Towers, Leaders and Maps (`openEncyclopedia()` in `src/site-template.html`).
- **Health in fights:** every unit shows a health ring (strategic view, 2D) or bar once it's hurt; small units (under 500 health) get a thinner one.
- **Range on hover:** with the mouse over a unit, on either board, while deploying or in a fight, a dotted circle in its side's colour shows how far it reaches. Tapping a squad shows the same ring (and opens its sheet), a squad just placed shows it at once, and in a fight the body tapped shows it too. Both rings are drawn in an SVG over the board (`boardMarks()` in `src/site-template.html`, which also draws the tutorial's blinking tiles) from one measure: `Game.squadReach` for a squad (its skills, Eagle eye, the map's cut and Test Grid's +2 m, a flame cone's 1.2× reach; an enemy's as far as this screen may know it: the skills and awakening you have seen, a disguise as what it pretends to be), `Game.towerReach` for a tower, and for a fight's body its live reach as the engine has it (Flow state stacks, Sightweavers' cut). Before, hover and tap measured differently (the tap ring left out flame cones, the hover ring Test Grid's +2 m and Eagle eye).
- **Level all:** a squad's sheet offers Level all when the gold covers two or more level-ups of that unit's squads: it levels them most XP first, one level a squad per pass (a squad with XP for another level gets it on the next pass), until the gold runs out ("Level all 2 of 3" when it can't do them all). `Game.levelAllPlan` / `levelAll`; it is one `levelUp` per squad, so a guest's Level all reaches the host the same way single level-ups do.
- **Skip to result** asks first: the first tap turns it into "Sure? Tap again to skip" for 3 s, like selling.
- **Match over:** Look at board hides the end screen so the final board can be looked at (hovered, and turned on the 3D board); Show result in the panel brings it back.
- **Intent:** tapping one of your squads while deploying draws an arrow from it to the enemy squad it means to go for first, with a dashed ring round that squad, but only when that squad already stands within its awareness where the fight begins (its range + sight, at least the minimum sight: what the engine spots at once). A pick further off depends on walking there first while both sides move and the opponent's new squads (unseen) can get in the way, so it shows as a dashed arrow the way the squad walks, with no unit named. Two arrows at most, with a small count of bodies on each when it splits. Its sheet says it in words. A unit picked in the shop previews the tile under the mouse or finger (dragging a card), and a squad dragged to another tile previews that tile. It is worked out by `Game.intentPreview` (`src/game.js`): a short fight played by the real engine from what this screen may see (`Game.seenBy`: the opponent's squads from earlier rounds where `seenAt` has them, nothing they did this round), until each body has picked an enemy to walk to, then grouped by enemy squad; each board state is worked out once and kept. It shows intent, not who wins, how much damage is done or how the fight ends. In a fight a tap on a unit (either side) shows its card and where its squad's bodies are walking right now, with small rings on whom they attack: the engine keeps whom a body walks to (`b.intent`) apart from whom it attacks (`b.lock`), which can differ. `node tools/tests/intent.js` checks that the preview picks exactly what the real fight picks when nothing is hidden; `tools/tests/intent-ui.js` checks the page on both boards.
- **Awareness** (`docs/AWARENESS.md`, `AWARENESS` in `src/game.js`) is part of the core fight, on in every gamemode: an engaged body picks whom to walk to within its sight by distance, its lane and crowding (melee slots), keeps its current one unless another is clearly better, and a new attack target skips an enemy its side is already about to kill. Counter Lab's sandbox fights stay as they were.
- **The tray (UI 2.0):** on a phone the deploy panel is a tray under the board that never covers it: gold, deployment pips with a +1 chip, the clock and Fight / Ready in one row, a hint line, then the shop grouped by tier (a small tier tab with the price, the units you have unlocked, and a + Unlock space while that tier has units left; it opens that tier's locked units). Drag a card up onto your zone to place it (a tap on the card, then a tile, also works; a hold opens its sheet; with a mouse the wheel or a sideways drag scrolls the tray). Drag a squad from this round down onto the tray to take it back, or an older one to sell it (asks first); dragging an older squad to another tile is the Move (50 gold, once a round). Swipe the tray down (or tap the handle on its top edge) to fold it to its top row and see more of the board; swipe up or tap SHOP to open it again (it opens by itself at the start of each round). The tutorial keeps its ✕ marks. Plan: `docs/UI2-PLAN.md`; standalone prototype: `ui2-test.html`.
- **Play:** open the site and tap Challenge a friend. Send the link, and your friend opens it in any browser. No accounts needed.
- **Counter Lab** (`lab.html`): a balance sandbox for designing units. Units saved there show up in the game under Load units on the same device.
- **3D test** (`lab3d.html`): real battles on the 3D board, with low-poly units, to try the 3D renderer and camera. You deploy blue against the AI in the strategic view (or set Blue: AI to watch): pick a unit in the shop and tap a tile, tap a squad from this round to take it back, drag it to move it, Auto lets the AI deploy for you. Blue has unlimited gold here. You can also place and drag in the angled view while deploying; there a tap places instead of going back, so use the Strategic / Angled / Ground buttons (or pinch out, scroll, Esc). Strategic view straight down; spread two fingers (or scroll) for the angled and ground-level views, drag to look around, twist (or right/middle drag, Q/E) to rotate, tap (or Esc) to go back. Fight, Next round and New armies drive the match.

## How live matches work

- Phones connect directly over WebRTC using [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com) and its free public matchmaking server. There is no backend of our own.
- The host's phone runs the match and decides each round. The battle engine only uses arithmetic that gives identical results in every browser, so both phones play back the same fight.
- Each round has a deploy countdown: 60 seconds in round 1, 30 after that. The fight starts when it runs out, or earlier when both players tap Ready.
- If either phone drops the page, opening the link again rejoins the match. The host's match is saved on the host's device.
- **Fight speed:** both phones play a fight at the same speed. A player's pick (1×, 1.5×, 2×) is what they want, and the fight plays at the slower of the two picks: a faster pick is a request ("Opponent wants 2×", that button glowing on the other phone) that takes hold on both once the other picks it too; a slower pick slows both at once. Picks carry over to the next fights of the match; a new match starts at 1×. Against the computer the buttons just set the speed. (`liveSpeed`, `playSpeed`, `showSpeed` in `src/site-template.html`.)
- **Switching away mid-fight** (another tab or app) pauses the replay where it is; coming back goes on from there. In a live match the other player waits on the results screen meanwhile (the next round needs both), so after 45 s away (`LIVE_AWAY`) the rest of the fight plays out to its result, as before. `tools/tests/playback-mp.js` checks both.
- **Fair turns:** the engine gives neither side the edge in a tie. The side whose bodies act first alternates every tick; both sides move from where everyone stood when the tick began; a body killed this tick still attacks; heals, lifesteal, Glut, range cuts, knockback, stealth reveals and a fallen tower's penalty take hold once the tick's attacks have landed; side 1's squads stand mirrored (front row first); each side draws dodges and random heals from its own stream. Mirrored armies come out as exact mirrors (`node tools/tests/fairness.js`); before, the host won about 60% of mirrored duels that weren't draws.

## Editing

The source is in `src/`. After changing it, run `node src/build.js` from the repo root. This rebuilds `index.html`, `lab.html` and `lab3d.html`.

- `engine.js`: the battle engine. Plain arithmetic only, so both phones in a live match play the same fight. Draws nothing.
- `game.js`: match rules (gold, rounds, maps, characters, the AI, what each player may see of the other). Draws nothing.
- `shapes.js`: how a unit looks: its shape, colour and 3-letter tag.
- `art.js`: the shared palette, coats of arms and tower icons, used on the board and in the panels.
- `camera3d.js`: the 3D camera and its touch, mouse and key controls (strategic, tilted, angled and ground views, in that order: spreading two fingers or scrolling goes one step in, pinching or scrolling back one step out; tilted shows your side of the board up close at 50°, angled is lower and further back at 26°). The tilted view can be rotated round the middle of the board like the cinematic views (twist two fingers, right/middle drag, Q/E); the turn carries on into the views further in and goes back to none at the strategic view. It also zooms in on the final seconds of a high-stakes fight (the battle cam). In the strategic and tilted views (and the angled view while the player is placing) the page can take taps and one-finger drags for itself, to place and move squads.
- `render3d.js`: draws the board and the battles in 3D (three.js), through the same `view()` and calls as `render2d.js`, with the same markers (health, wind-up and flank timers, statuses, charisma, levels, buffs, tags, badges, placement hints, coats of arms, the elf spires, ice walls and hail, the crippled-army notice). The map floor is painted by `render2d.js` (its `floor()` call) and laid on the ground. Markers follow the camera: health as rings in the strategic view and bars in the angled view; the ground view is purely cinematic (no markers, no zones or grid on the floor).
- `models3d.js`: the low-poly models: units (soldier, archer, caster, goblin, brute, flier, horse, spider, airship), picked from each unit's stats, and their own models for the tier 4 units (Ether Golem, Sun Priest, Meteor Caller, War Mammoth, Necromancer, Truespear), Ent, Rogue, Sniper, Slimeshots, Skeleton Archers, the Obelisk (and its awakened eye) and the wizards (each with its element on the staff, the Frost Wizard an ice crystal), a tower for each tower type and one for faction towers (in the faction's colour), the elf spire and the thrown spear.
- `lab3d.js` + `lab3d-template.html`: the 3D test page: runs matches against the AI, with blue's deploy in the 3D view, and shows them with `render3d.js`.
- `render2d.js`: draws the board and the battles on the canvas. It reads what to show only through the `view()` the page hands it, so another renderer (3D) can take its place.
- `site-template.html` + `game.css`: the game page: panels, menus, live matches, music. The menu playlist is every song that isn't deployment or battle music (`PLAYLISTS`, `MAP_MUSIC`), played in random order: a random song first, then another random one each time a song ends, never the same twice in a row (a song picked in the music sheet plays on its own instead). On a phone in a match the board fills the screen and the deploy panel is a drawer docked at the bottom, slid up or down on a blue pull tab (Settings → Phone layout: Below the board brings back the panel under the board); picking a unit puts it down and shows a line over the board with an ✕ to stop placing. On a PC the panel sits beside the board. Its board input works on tiles, so the 2D and the 3D board share it. The game starts on the 3D board; the in-match Settings (the cog) switch between it and the 2D board, saved on the device. `?board=3d` in the address forces 3D for testing, `?board=2d` starts on 2D. Only then does it load three.js (`THREE_URL`: `vendor/` here, jsDelivr in the artifact) and the 3D code from `src/`, and it falls back to the 2D board with one line when 3D can't load or start, the phone drops it, or fights run too slowly. The 3D board draws only while something moves; when forced for testing it shows a speed meter in its top right corner (tap it to change the quality).
- `template.html`: Counter Lab.

`vendor/` holds three.js (0.180.0, MIT licence), used by the 3D test page.

## Test Grid map

A test map (Test Grid, only when picked, never random) on a bigger board: 14 columns by 18 rows, so each player's zone is 14 by 7 tiles instead of 12 by 6 (no man's land stays 4 rows). The towers are mirrored as on the Mushroom Cavern (`pointMirror`): the second player's tower stands on the flipped column.

Treasuries (the map's `treasury`, `TREASURY` in `src/game.js`): from round 1 each player has a treasury, 400 health, on the other side of their zone from their tower, one column in toward the middle and one row forward of the tower. Its tile is kept free from round 1 (drawn faintly until then). Like the tower it stands again at full health in every fight and doesn't count as army; losing it doesn't cripple anything. A fight it survives pays its owner 50 gold; if it is destroyed, the opponent steals 25% of the gold its owner left unspent at the fight, to the nearest 50, at least 50 and at most 200 (`treasurySteal`), instead (no pay that round). The results screen lists both. Tapping a treasury (yours or theirs) explains what it does. `tools/tests/treasury.js` checks it. The Factions devtest plays on this map too, so it has the treasuries as well. Units on it have 2 m more range (melee units, under 5 m, keep theirs), and units with a move speed of 3 or more move 0.2 m/s slower, 0.4 from 5 on; the Shiv and the Water Mage keep theirs (the map's `rangeAdd`, `slowSteps` and `tuneKeep`, applied by `mapTuned()`). The size is the map's `grid` in `MAPS` (`src/game.js`); `Game.useGrid(m)` puts the board on a match's grid, and every exported function that takes a match does it first, so all other maps keep the standard 12 by 16 board. The page's `syncGrid()` resizes the 2D board and starts the 3D board again when the grid changes.

## Bridge map and terrain

The Bridge map (only when picked, never random) was made in the map editor: the Test Grid's board, towers, treasuries, flanks and tuning, with two rivers (columns 5 and 8, rows 6 to 11) and a raised bridge between them. Its editor JSON is kept as it came out of the editor in `EDITOR_MAPS` (`src/game.js`) and hangs on the map as `terrain`; another editor map goes in the same way (its buildings must still be on the game's own spots for now). What the terrain does (`TERRAIN`, `terrainTile`, `terrainOf` in `src/game.js`; the terrain section of `createBattle` in `src/engine.js`):

- **Walls** (`#`) stop walking, shots and sight; **water** (`~`) stops walking only (shots and sight pass over it); **shallow water** (`s`) slows whoever wades in it by 30%; a **bush** (`b`) hides whoever stands in it (seen from 3 m, or from inside a bush within a tile) and stops sight through it further than a tile from the one looking. Nothing can be placed on a wall or water tile. A tile with a form from the editor (line, corner, diagonal, edge wall) has only that much of it. Objectives and mushroom tiles aren't played yet.
- **Walking round things:** the engine works on a grid of 2 m cells (3 x 3 a tile). A walking body checks the next 9 m of its way; while it is clear it walks as before, and when a wall or water is in the way it follows the shortest way round (a path over the cell grid, kept per goal across fights) toward the furthest point of it it can walk to in a straight line, so its heading swings round in a smooth curve to the nearest opening. A body pushed into a blocked cell (by allies, knockback, a drop) steps back out.
- **Sight:** an enemy behind a wall can't be spotted, picked or shot. A body with no enemy in sight keeps marching, and once nothing is left ahead it walks round to the nearest one it can't see, without stopping for it. Lines of sight are worked out per pair of cells once and kept, so walls cost very little: AI matches on a walled version of the map ran at about 0.3 ms a fight tick, as fast as open ground.
- **Height:** a level per tile, −2 to +2. A body on higher ground than its target gets +1 m range and +2% damage per level of difference (`Engine.HEIGHT`), so +2 shooting at −2 gets +4 m and +8%. Lower ground costs nothing. Flying units ignore height (and walls and water).
- **Drawing:** both boards draw the water, walls, shallow water and bushes on the floor, and height as light and shade with the slopes lit. `Game.groundHeight` gives the drawn height (1.5 m a level): it slopes smoothly between the middles of walkable tiles, so raised ground meets the open ground round it in a ramp, while water sits 0.6 m lower with a bank on its side. The 3D board raises its floor by it, stands models, rings, markers and shots on it, and draws wall tiles as stone blocks.

`node tools/tests/terrain.js` checks the map, placement, AI matches on it (nobody ever stands in the water) and, on a small made-up grid, walking round a wall, walls blocking shots and the height bonus.

## Shiv, Water Mage and bleed

- **Unit traits:** a unit's `trait: { name, text }` shows on its sheet as "Trait: name". Shiv: Apply bleed; Water Mage: Waterhealing; Steel Wizard: Steel armor (6 armor).
- **Either-or skills:** `skillOr: [[1, 2]]` on a unit means a player can own skill 2 or skill 3 of it, not both (`Game.skillOr`, `canBuySkill`); the sheet boxes them together with an OR between them. The Shiv, Water Mage, Steel Wizard and Earth Wizard have it.
- **Upgrade rows** (`upRow()` in `src/site-template.html`): every upgrade (unit skills, tower skills, mage generals, the loan, the Mecha Tower's bots, building skills, the altar) is one compact row: its name and button, then one line of what it does, cut short. Press and hold a row (or Enter on it) for the whole text and details (what it needs, who it's for, the price rule). Choices that rule each other out are boxed with an OR between them (`orGroup()`): either-or skills, the mage generals, a building's skill of the round.
- **Tap a unit in a fight** (`inspectCard()`): a card at the top left of the board shows that body's health now, level, hit, attacks a second, range, speed, armor or ward, how many of its squad are left, the upgrades its squad fights with (bought skills, Enzo's free one, an awakening; built-in ones left out), and what's on it (stunned, poisoned, bleeding, burning, slowed, summoning). It updates live and goes when the body falls, a tap elsewhere or its ✕. Stealthed enemies can't be picked.
- **Bleed** (`BLEED` in `src/engine.js`): a unit with `bleedEvery: 2` gives a stack of bleed with every second hit it lands on the same target. A bleeding body loses 3 health twice a second for 2.5 s (each new stack starts the 2.5 s again; 48 a tick at 5 stacks); every stack doubles that, up to 5 stacks; at 4 stacks it also loses 2% of its max health a second, and at 5 stacks 5% (`BLEED.pct`). Bleed goes past armor. The stacks are the target's own, so every source adds to them. Too deep (`bleedDeep`): 1 s longer, and no healing of any kind while it lasts. Anemia (`bleedStun`): a stun of 1.2 s when the stacks reach 5. Titanium spears give a stack too. Drawn as a red drop with a pip per stack (2D) or a red dot per stack (3D).
- **Water Mage:** `waterHeal`: allies in its splash heal for its hit, and with no enemy in reach it shoots the most wounded ally in reach. Pool shaper (`pools`): its attacks leave pools for the rest of the fight that never overlap; every second enemies in one take 25% of the hit, allies heal it and get +0.5 m/s for 2 s. Flow state (`flow`): +0.1 attacks a second and +0.4 m range per attack made standing still, up to 5; walking takes them away.
- **Steel Wizard:** Heavy metal (+4 armor, +25% health), then Reforge (`aura`: +2 armor; allies within 8 m get +20 on every hit and +0.1 attacks a second) or Shape Titanium spears (`spears`: spends 1 armor per spear, 1.5 s to forge, 200 damage at 35 m/s and a stack of bleed).
- **Earth Wizard:** +25% health and 2% regeneration, then Stone spikes (+5 m range, two shots in quick succession, 20% slower) or Living armor (`slam`: +8 armor, −13 m range, a cone slam for 200 damage that stuns for 1 s; its cone is 25° each side, narrower than the Flame Wizard's 30°, set by the skill's own `cone`).
- Poisoned units heal 50% less (`POISON_HEAL`; slime units still drink poison in full) and move 20% slower while the poison lasts (`POISON_SLOW`; the stronger of this and any other slow counts; not slime units or Berserkers). `node tools/tests/poison-slow.js` checks the slow.
- `node tools/tests/shiv-water.js` checks all of this headlessly.

## The Assassin, Awaken skills and the unit rework

- **Master and Awaken skills:** units have 4 skill slots (`Engine.SKILL_SLOTS`). The 4th is a Master skill or an Awaken skill: `skillNeedsAny: [null, null, null, [1, 2]]` on the unit means it needs skill 2 or skill 3 first (`Game.skillNeedsAny`; skill 2 or 3 can't be taken back while it leans on it), and `flat: true` on the skill keeps it at its own price (300) without making the unit's other skills dearer (`Game.isFlat`).
  - A **Master skill** (`master: true` on the skill, `Game.isMaster`) is bought once and works for every squad of the unit, like any skill: the Sniper's Overwatch, the Wisps' Spirit walk, the Brute's Berserkers.
  - An **Awaken skill** (any other flat skill, `Game.isAwaken`) is bought for one squad at a time, on that squad's sheet (`Game.awaken`, `undoAwaken`, `canAwaken`, `awakenBlock`): the squad keeps `sq.awaken` (the round it was bought) and only it gets the skill (`Game.unitFor` with the squad). It is hidden from the opponent until the fight (`Game.seenAwake`; the guest's sync drops it, the host's sheet of a guest squad too). A squad taken back refunds its awakening, a sold one counts it in its worth. A player can awaken only one unit type a match (`Game.wokeUnit`): any number of squads of the type they awakened first, none of another (`awakenBlock` says `'type'`); it holds from the fight on (`m.woke`, set in `resolve`), even once those squads are sold, and taking back this round's awakening frees the choice again. The host drops a guest's awakenings of another type. The Ember Imps' Summon hellfiend and the Leaf Wizard's Leafblade (the Ent's Explode on death is a Master skill now). An awakened squad has a gold ring round its bodies (2D) or on its tile (3D). The AI awakens squads now and then; Claude (Play Claude) gets `"awaken": [squad ids]`.
  - The sheet labels them Master or Awaken and says what they need.
- **Skills with more than one effect:** a skill can carry `also: [{ type, params }, ...]`, applied and described after its own effect (Longbones, Graverot arrows, Dread rangers, Forward planting, Bullhead, Sightweavers, Shadowborn, Overwatch).
- **Assassin** (tier 4, 500 gold, 1 body, 350 health, 500 hit, 0.35 attacks a second, 6 m/s, melee): stealthed (`stealth` on the unit), dashes the last 10 m to its target at 30 m/s without being seen (`dash`), deals 50% to buildings (`bldgPct`), and every hit it lands gives the target 2 stacks of bleed (Bloodrush, `bleedHit`; its second trait, `trait2`; with Death Flurry every blow does). Silent blade (its first attack doesn't give it away), then Death Flurry (3 blows of 200, 0.2 attacks a second; a kill leaps it to the nearest enemy within 10 m for the next blow) or Shadow hunter (hunts bodies with no ally within 3 m, `LONE_R`; none for 5 s and it fights as usual until its next lone kill). With either of the two it ignores the enemy tower (`noTower`): it never picks, leaps to or hunts the tower while any other enemy can be picked (the treasury, watchtowers, walls and buildings aren't the tower). With nothing else left to pick (say only a Sniper safe inside that tower, or stealthed units) it attacks the tower after all, so a fight never stalls on it.
- **Goblin Swarm:** AAARGH! (+0.8 m/s, +0.3 attacks a second), then Tacticalness (+3 bodies, spread) or More where that came from (each kill makes a new goblin, `killSpawn`).
- **Brute:** Tuff skin (+25% health, 4 armor), then Rampager (+0.4 m/s and +0.2 attacks a second per kill, up to 4) or Bullhead (+4 armor; its 1st, 4th, 7th... attack charges through its target and 6 m on at twice its speed, 100 damage and a tile of knockback to each body in the way, +4 armor while it runs: `bull`).

## Towers: Keep and Bastion

- **Keep, Guards:** the 4 guards stand one on each side of the keep (front, behind, left, right) for 5 s. A guard that is hit leaves its post at once and goes for whoever hit it (`b.guard` and `b.avenge` in `src/engine.js`).
- **Bastion:** its attack is a moat of fire (`MOAT` in `src/game.js`: 12 damage 4 times a second to every enemy within 5 m); it has no shot of its own. **Home ground** also gives it a shield that blocks its first 2 hits each fight. **Defensive walls** (250 gold, replacing Moats) gives the bastion +4 armor and 3 walls (`BWALL`), each along one edge of a tile of your own half of the board (not the board's outer edge), from the tower's sheet: tap near a tile's edge and the wall goes along it (`m.bwalls[side] = [{ col, row, edge }]`, `Game.EDGES`; an edge is the same from either of its tiles, `bwallOn`; an older wall without an edge stands on its tile's front edge). A wall takes no tile: squads stand on both sides of it. In fights a wall is a long thin barrier (`u.barrier` and `u.barrierY`, its half length and half thickness, in `src/engine.js`): enemies can't walk into it (they walk up and hit it) and their shots at anything behind it hit the wall instead (lobbed shells fly over); its own side walks and shoots through. `tools/tests/bastion.js` checks all of this.

## Factions gamemode

A second gamemode on the standard map. Each faction is a small roster with its own theme, a trait and its own tower. Before the match the players draft factions one at a time, two each (A, B, B, A). Each player can then only unlock, buy and place units of their own two factions, and at round 3 they pick between their two factions' towers instead of the usual tower offer. Starting packages are dealt from your own factions' units, so give every faction at least one unit at 200 gold or less.

The mode unlocks on the start screen (Factions vs AI) and in the friend lobby (Standard / Factions) once there are enough factions for a draft (4 for now; each pick and ban needs its own).

Factions go in `src/factions.json`, then run `node tools/counter-table.js` and rebuild. Format:

```json
{
  "factions": [{
    "id": "deepsea", "name": "Deep Sea", "color": "#3FA7D6",
    "theme": "One line shown in the draft",
    "trait": { "name": "Crushing depths", "text": "+20% damage, -15% speed", "hit": 20, "speed": -15 },
    "tower": { "name": "Coral Spire", "text": "What it does", "unit": { "hp": 650, "hit": 30, "aps": 1.2, "range": 10 } },
    "units": [ { "name": "...", "cost": 100, "bodies": 4, "hp": 200, "...": "same fields as units in default-roster.json" } ]
  }],
  "skills": []
}
```

- `trait` changes all the faction's units (spawned ones too) by a percentage: `hp`, `hit`, `aps`, `range`, `speed`. It is optional.
- `tower.unit` overrides the standard tower's stats. With `drone: { orbit, unit }` (and `idle: true`) the tower doesn't shoot itself: a drone circles it `orbit` metres out and does the shooting (the Mecha Tower).
- `tower.awake`: the tower's stats while Core Awoken is on (the Mecha Tower drives and lobs mortar shells).
- `tower.rounds`: skills bought every round for that round's fight, each `{ id, name, cost, text, bots: { count, unit }, mines: { each, hit, radius } }` (the Mecha Tower's minebots and repairbots; a bot unit has `bot: "mine"` or `"repair"`).
- `skills`: skills only the factions use. Units can also use any skill from `default-roster.json` by its id.
- Unit names must be unique across all factions.
- The draft order, picks per player and the map are in `FACTION_MODE` in `src/game.js`. A ban phase later is a matter of adding `{ "who": "A", "act": "ban" }` steps to its order.
- `node tools/tests/factions.js` checks the draft and faction matches headlessly with six throwaway test factions.
- `node tools/tests/tutorial.js` checks headlessly that tutorial rounds 1 and 2 come out the same wherever the player places.
- `node tools/tests/standard-pack.js` checks the Standard pack (units, leaders, packages, towers), the bank's one action a round (a loan or the Major investment) and Frozen Peak with its extras off, headlessly.

### Factions – devtest

A test bed for the Factions mode while it's being designed: the Test Grid board, a draft where both players may take the same faction, no tower until round 3, a free pick every round (lesser buildings, the Powertower, Powers, neutral units), spells, arrows that guide a squad, and disguised units. vs AI only. The plan, who does what and the data formats are in [docs/FACTIONS-DEVTEST.md](docs/FACTIONS-DEVTEST.md); notes on unit awareness and targeting in [docs/AWARENESS.md](docs/AWARENESS.md).
- Until there are 4 factions the mode stays locked. To try a faction's units now: Counter Lab → Import units → "From <faction> (faction)", save, then Load units in the game.

Fight mechanics added for factions (they work for any unit, in Counter Lab too):

- `roll` (seconds): a roller. It never stops to attack; it rolls at its target from 1 m/s up to its `speed` over this many seconds, hits every enemy body it rolls over for its `hit`, carries on a tile past the target, stops and rolls back. `aps` and `range` only matter for the shop's numbers.
- `build`: `{ "time": 1.5, "range": 25, "count": 1, "unit": { ...stats } }`. Once per fight the unit stops when an enemy is within `range` − 1 m, builds `count` copies of `unit` (a tile apart) in `time` seconds and fights on. Builders building next to each other speed each other up (1×, 1.5×, 1.75×, …). What it builds never moves, levels with its builder, earns the builder's squad its XP, and doesn't count as a unit left standing.
- `skillNeeds`: `[null, null, [0, 1]]` = skill 3 can only be bought once skills 1 and 2 are owned (empty slots are skipped).
- Skill types: `momentum` (a roller gets up to speed faster), `link` (a squad's bodies share one health pool and fall together), `healbot` (a construct follows each squad and heals it a share of max health per second), `builder` (more constructs per build, faster builds, better constructs).
- Buildings are listed under `"buildings"` in `src/factions.json`: `{ id, name, faction, hp, buildSecs, selfBuild, survive, rebuild, levelCost, color, text, awake, skills }`. A player gets one in two ways:
  - a unit skill of type `building` (`"params": { "building": "factory" }`): buying it places the building on a free tile of your zone (the Factory);
  - a unit with `"building": "altar"` bought in the shop like any unit (the Altar of Build): a squad that never moves; `footprint: 2` makes it take a 2x2 block (its col, row are the block's first ones). Give it `idle`, `noCount` and `anchored` too, so it doesn't fight in Counter Lab; the counter table scores it 0.
- A building is a site until it is built: the nearest builder without a job builds it during fights (`buildSecs` seconds of work; a site nobody builds builds itself at `selfBuild` times that speed). The work carries over between fights; with `survive` a site still standing at the end of a fight it was worked on is built at once. A built building that falls starts the next round at `rebuild` %. Once built, one of its `skills` can be bought each round: `unit` (one unit made `time` s into the fight), `make` (`[[building, its skill, count], ...]`), `bless` (Blessing of the Cycle: `build`, `hp`, `speed`, `aps` %, `regen` for all your constructs) or `awake` (Core Awoken: your buildings get their kind's `awake` stats and walk; `awake.built` changes what builders build). What it makes earns it XP; it levels up with gold, and its units with it.
- New unit fields: `spinCap` (how far spin-up goes; default 3×), `tag` (its 3-letter tag on the board), `smooth` (no bobbing in 3D), `flee` (metres: keeps away from enemies that close, still shooting), `backline` (shoots the farthest enemy in reach), `lob` (shells fly over whoever is in the way and only land where aimed).
