Units

Raw strength is total health × total damage per second for what the gold buys. Units with equal raw strength are equal on paper; range, splash, armor and ward are what turn them into counters.

Skills

Each unit has two skill slots. In the game, players buy a skill once and it applies to all their squads of that unit. Several units can share a skill. To tune a skill for one unit only, open Adjust in that unit’s skill slot: whatever you change there applies to that unit alone, and everything else still follows the skill here. Set which skills a unit has in its card under Units, and tick On in tests to include a skill in the counter table, the combos and the price check.

How a fight works

Targeting and movement

Every body walks toward the nearest enemy and stops once it is in range. Melee units have a range of about 2 m.

Overkill

Damage beyond a body's remaining health is wasted. Big hits on small bodies lose most of their damage.

Splash

Every enemy within the splash radius of the target takes the full hit. Packed squads take many hits from one attack.

Armor

Subtracts a flat amount from every hit, down to the armor floor. It shuts down small, fast hits and barely dents big ones.

Ward

No single hit can deal more than the ward value. It shuts down big hits and ignores small ones.

Range vs speed

A ranged unit gets free shots while the enemy closes the gap. Fast units shrink that window.

Burst and shotgun

A burst fires its shots 0.15 s apart along the line toward its first target. Each shot hits the first body in its path: the target while it lives, then whoever stands behind it. If the line is empty, the shot misses. A shotgun fires all its pellets at once into a 60° cone, each at a different body, wrapping back to bodies already hit when it runs out.

Flame cone

Like the Vulcan: every attack hits all enemies in range inside a cone toward the target, stealthed ones too, instantly. Damage grows with how many enemies stand in the fire, so it melts swarms, while armor eats a big share of each small tick and longer-ranged units can shoot it before it gets close.

Wind-up

The first time an enemy is in range, the unit sets up for the wind-up time, like a sniper lying down. Once set up, it fires at its normal rate at anything in range. If it has to move, it gets up, and it sets up again when an enemy is next in range. Moving during the setup cancels it.

Spin-up

Raises attack speed every second the unit keeps firing, up to three times its base speed.

Pierce and projectiles

A piercing shot also hits bodies lined up behind its target. A projectile with a speed travels in a straight line and hits the first enemy it touches, so units moving sideways can dodge it while units charging straight at it get hit.

Upgrades

Each upgrade adds the unit’s starting health and hit again: one upgrade doubles them, two triple them. In the game, squads earn upgrades through experience and gold; here you set them directly to test upgraded matchups.

Skills

Upgrades a player buys during a match. Each unit has two. A skill changes the unit’s stats, gives it a new ability, or both. Poison ignores armor and ward. Spawned bodies fight like normal bodies but don’t count for damage after the round.

Combos

A pair fights as one army: the shorter-ranged unit in front, the other a short gap behind it, each with the gold per side. Its opponent gets twice the gold. Synergy compares the pair’s result with the average of its two units’ results against the same opponent.

Fair price and tiers

The price check finds how many squads of a unit tie against 6 squads of each 100-gold unit, and turns that into a fair price in gold. Under 140 gold is tier 1, 140–280 is tier 2, above 280 is tier 3. Raising one stat and measuring again shows what that stat is worth for that unit.

Minimum range, charge and knockback

A unit can’t attack anything inside its minimum range. Charge multiplies the next hit after the unit has run 5 m. Knockback pushes a hit body away, fully for small bodies and barely for big ones.
